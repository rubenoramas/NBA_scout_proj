1. Data

Loading the data date...

The running app reads only MongoDB. It never calls the NBA site, ESPN or hoopR. R scripts on a laptop pull the data into seed files in data/seed/, and a loader fills MongoDB from them when Docker starts. Loading twice changes nothing.

RouteWhat it givesNetwork
1. hoopR pre-pulled NBA stats files (load_nba_stats_*)Main route: schedule, box scores, shots, player and team stats, tracking tables, play types, play-by-play, game lineups, per-game matchups, five-man lineups, combine measurementsAny network. Each file holds a season and loads in about a second.
2. ESPN (load_nba_*)Only steals: ESPN's play-by-play names the stealer; the NBA's does not. Stealers are matched to NBA ids by name, with team as a tie-breaker.Any network.
3. The live NBA stats site (nba_*)Optional extras only (see Known limits). The app works without them.No answer from campus Wi-Fi, so it is pulled only from a phone hotspot.

Season arguments differ. load_nba_stats_* takes the season's start year (2026 for 2026-27), ESPN takes the end year (2027), and live functions take "2026-27". Without the arrow package most files load empty.

Refresh. ./refresh.sh runs every pull script in order, reloads MongoDB and prints the row count change per seed file. The Refresh data button does the same through an optional container: docker compose --profile refresh up --build.

Freshness. The top bar shows the latest game in the data and the pull time. The dot turns amber when the pull is more than 2 days old.

Checks run on the data are listed with each feature below, for example points summing to every final score (On/off) and passer names matching a teammate on the floor (97.4% of 13,732 named passers, Nov 25).

2. Shot zones

Rule in pulls/pull_shots.R

The files give each shot's x and y (tenths of a foot, basket at 0, 0; baseline at y = -47.5) and whether it was a 2 or a 3. The zone is derived:

ZoneRule
Restricted area2-pointer and sqrt(x^2 + y^2) <= 40
Paint (non-RA)other 2-pointer and |x| <= 80 and y <= 142.5
Midrangeevery other 2-pointer
Corner three3-pointer and y <= 92.5
Above the breakevery other 3-pointer

Validation, Nov 18, 36,829 shots: the distance from x and y was within 1 ft of the distance written in the description for all 33,520 shots that state one; "3PT" in the description agreed with the 2-or-3 field on every shot; the nearest corner three was 22.1 ft out. Side of the floor (left or right) is not shown: see Known limits.

3. Ratings and badges

Computed in app/model/ratings.py at load time

Twelve 0 to 99 ratings, regular season only, this season blended with last season.

StepFormula
1. Blendw = MIN_this / (MIN_this + 600); blended rate = w x rate_this + (1 - w) x rate_last (the sample and the league rate blend the same way)
2. Shrink oncevalue = (rate x sample + k x league) / (sample + k); k = 50 for field goal types, 30 for free throws, 25 possessions for post-ups
Per 36 ratesshrunk per 36 = (count + league rate per minute x 400) / (minutes + 400) x 36
3. Qualify200+ minutes this season, or 500+ last season
4. Percentileseach component ranked among qualified players, 0 to 1 (flipped where lower is better)
5. Ratingrating = round(99 x percentile rank of the weighted average of component percentiles)

Tooltips show the player's real rate beside the adjusted one. A rating is tagged as a small sample below a threshold per attribute (for example 40 restricted-area attempts for Finishing, 50 threes for Three-point shooting, 300 minutes for Playmaking). USG% is shown as a stat with a league rank, not a rating, because it describes role, not skill.

BadgeRule (both seasons pooled, 500+ minutes)
Deep Range40+ threes from 28 to 39 ft (heaves of 40+ ft left out), 20%+ of his threes from 28+ ft, and a deep 3P% at or above the league's overall 3P%.
Streaky Shooter300+ shots that followed another shot in the same game. A one-sided two-proportion z-test that his make rate after a make beats his rate after a miss, with p below 0.05 divided by the number of players tested (Bonferroni).
On-Ball Pest15+ lost-ball steals (ESPN play-by-play), top 10% of qualified players in lost-ball steals per 36, and deflections per 36 above the median.
Poster MakerDriving, running and cutting dunks only (alley-oops and putbacks left out): 25+ of them and top 10% in those dunks per 36.

The Streaky Shooter test:

z = (p_after_make - p_after_miss) / sqrt(p (1 - p) (1/n_make + 1/n_miss)), with p the pooled make rate

With about 270 players tested, a plain 0.05 threshold would hand out about 13 badges by luck alone, so the threshold is split across all of them. Comparing make rates after makes and misses within the same games is slightly biased against finding streakiness (Miller and Sanjurjo, 2018), so the test is conservative. Players who were tested and did not pass show "Tested: not streaky".

Badge names and art are our own, not NBA 2K's. Every name lives in app/model/badge_config.py.

4. Matchup model

app/model/matchups.py · validation Nov 19

Units are points per 100 partial possessions, the time a defender spends guarding a scorer. The league averages about 24. Seasons blend like the ratings.

PartFormula
League rateL = total points / total partial possessions
Scorer's normB = (pts + 200 x L) / (poss + 200)
Defender effectD = (points allowed - expected - his poss x P) / (his poss + 300); expected = sum of poss x B of each scorer he guarded; P = average (actual - expected) per possession at his position
Size fitS = b_h x height gap + b_w x weight gap / 10 lb + b_ws x wingspan gap (explains 0.1% of what is left, Nov 18)
Pair estimateE = (pair pts + 200 x mu) / (pair poss + 200), mu = B + D + S
AssignmentAll 120 orderings of our five against theirs; the lowest sum of USG% x E wins. A guard may not guard a center (or the reverse) without 20+ possessions of history.

Validation: trained on last season plus this season before Nov 1, tested on the 21,605 pairs and 105,863 partial possessions since. Correlation with actual points per 100, weighted by possessions:

Scorer's norm only (B)0.1620.330–

Position-relative, k = 500.1700.31536.2%

A same-position guess matches the top defender 43.3% of the time, and a random pick 20%. Calibration by fifths, predicted against actual: 15.2 / 16.1, 20.4 / 20.2, 23.8 / 23.1, 27.7 / 25.6, 35.2 / 36.5. k was picked on the same test window, so the result for k = 200 is slightly optimistic.

The position problem. Without P, centers averaged +5.3 per 100 and guards -1.5, because bigs are matched to scorers near the rim, so every guard looked like a better defender. Measuring each defender against his position (P on Nov 19: C +6.4, F -1.2, G -1.7) moved the averages to C -0.2, F -0.0, G -0.1. It costs a little raw prediction (0.181 to 0.178): the center offset is real in the data, but it reflects when bigs are matched, not how well they defend.

Known limits. Switches and help defense split every scorer across several defenders. A rim protector logged as the matched defender when he helps can rate poorly. "Who guarded him most" also measures coaches' choices, not what is best.

5. On/off

Section 6d of pulls/pull_stats.R · checks Nov 25

The play-by-play and the game lineups file line up row for row (all 842,527 rows over both seasons), so every play has the ten players on the floor. Regular season only.

ORtg = 100 x team points / team possessions; DRtg = 100 x opponent points / opponent possessions; net = ORtg - DRtg; on-off = net on - net off

  • Points: the running score is carried forward and each change goes to the ten players on the floor.
  • Free throws go to the ten players on the floor at the foul that caused them, not when they are shot. Last season's feed lists most substitutions during free throws before or between the shots, this season's after them, so crediting "when shot" treated the seasons differently. The foul rule moved 11,412 of 45,220 free throw points last season and 394 of 9,852 this season.
  • Possessions are counted, from the play-by-play's possession numbers (an offensive rebound keeps the number), and credited to the players on the floor when each one ends, with the same free throw rule.
  • "Off" counts only games he played, so an injury absence does not count as time off the floor.
  • Not shown under 250 possessions on the floor; a small sample under 1,000 on or off.
CheckResult (Nov 25)
Points equal the final scoreAll 1,230 games last season and all 261 this season
Possessions against paceCounted possessions per 48 correlate 0.998 with each team's pace in the team stats and run 0.86 lower on average (at most 1.09). The NBA's pace is an estimate; on the same games the box-score estimate gives 101.2 possessions per team per game against 99.1 counted.

6. Game preview

app/model/preview.py · validation Nov 25

margin = 2.0 + 0.92 x (home net - away net) - 0.05 x (home rest - away rest) - 2.6 x home back-to-back + 1.1 x away back-to-back

  • Net rating before the game comes only from games on earlier dates, blended with last season's final net rating: (g x current + 5 x last) / (g + 5), g = games played.
  • Rest is days since the previous scheduled game, capped at 3. Its effect is about zero once back-to-backs are known.
  • Fitted by least squares on last season's 1,230 regular-season games. A test changes a game's own result, a same-day game and a later game, and checks that the inputs do not move.
  • Two error numbers. The screen shows the average miss (11 points). The win probability uses the standard deviation of the misses: P(home wins) = Phi(margin / 14.6).

Validation: trained on 2025-26, tested on 2026-27 through Nov 24 (261 games).

7. Recent form

app/services/form.py · check Nov 25

Last 10 is each team's last 10 played regular-season games. Team stats are computed from the box scores with one function for any window, with possessions estimated as FGA + 0.44 x FTA - OREB + TOV, averaged over both teams. The Season view keeps the NBA's official numbers.

Box scores have no team turnovers (such as shot clock violations) or team rebounds, and the NBA's team game logs file matches the player sums exactly, so only the play-by-play has them. Last 10 therefore ranks every team's last 10 the same way, and shows "Season*", the team's season computed the same way, beside each value. Ratings, badges, matchups, on/off, rotations and the preview stay season numbers.

8. The AI

app/report/

Facts in, text out. The app builds one facts object from numbers it already computed, rounds every number once, and sends only that to Claude with a system prompt. The reply is constrained to JSON. The page shows the same facts beside the text, so every claim can be traced.

  • Use only the facts; every number must appear in them exactly as given; no outside knowledge.
  • Call out anything marked small sample, small history or last season only.
  • Describe where shots come from and how possessions end. Never which way a player drives, and never named set plays.
  • Plain sentences for coaches. No hype, no em dashes.
ItemValue
Modelclaude-sonnet-5-5 (Anthropic Messages API, effort medium, JSON schema output, server-side refusal fallback). REPORT_MODEL=claude-haiku-5-5 switches to Haiku 5.5.
Prompt versionsreport-v2 (team report, app/report/system_prompt.md), onepager-v1 ("How to guard him", app/report/onepager_prompt.md)
Cost and timeTeam report: about 3.3 cents, about 9,000 input and 1,450 output tokens, about 10 seconds (Nov 20). "How to guard him": about 3,650 input and 150 output tokens on average over 7 texts (Nov 25), cached so each page is written once per data date.
No API keyThe app starts and every non-AI page works. AI panels say to add ANTHROPIC_API_KEY to .env.

The number check (app/report/check.py) finds every number in the text and looks for it in the facts. Dates and seasons from the facts, counts from 1 to 5 and "per 100" are allowed; anything else is flagged on the page.

Measured limits (Nov 20): in 20 real reports (about 710 numbers per 10 reports) it fired 0 times. A test with made-up numbers showed why that is not proof: the facts hold about 66 of the 100 whole numbers from 0 to 99 (mostly ratings), so a random wrong whole number slips through about two times in three. A random wrong decimal like 38.2% is caught about 8 times in 10. That is why the facts stay visible beside the text.

9. Known limits

LimitWhat it means
No user accountsSaved lineups and reports are shared by everyone using one copy of the app. Anyone can edit or delete them.
Live-only itemsShots by dribbles and by defender distance, defended FG%, passes between teammates, dunk score, clutch stats, and NBA Cup standings and labels exist only on the live NBA site. They are optional and not in the app, so Tough Shot Maker and Closer badges are skipped.
Left and right not confirmedNothing in the files says whether positive x is the shooter's left or right. pulls/check_sides.R checks it against the live shot chart and must run from a network that reaches the NBA site, so sides stay off the screens.
No drive direction or set playsPublic data has neither. The app shows where shots were taken and how possessions end (play types), never which way a player drives.
Small samples early in a seasonRatings blend with last season, and ratings, matchups, on/off, lineups and Last 10 tag small samples. Nearly every on/off number this season is tagged.
Coverage gapsWingspan exists only for combine-measured players (309 of 519, Nov 18). Positions cover 503 of 519 (97%). Play-in games and the NBA Cup final have no box score in the files.
Team turnovers and reboundsNot in box scores, so computed TOV% and OREB% run below the official numbers (Recent form).
Neutral sitesThe schedule does not flag neutral-site games, so the game preview gives home court to the listed home team.
Name matchingESPN stealers are matched to NBA ids by name, with team as a tie-breaker and a small manual override table for names that never match. Play-by-play passers are matched by name to the four teammates on the floor (97.4% on Nov 25); the rest are left out.
RotationsThe lineup table keeps only the 2,000 five-man lineups with the most minutes league-wide (about 3+ minutes this season). Tags describe who is on the floor, not how the lineup played.
Data is a snapshotEverything reflects the last pull. The top bar shows the latest game in the data and when it was pulled.

10. Rotation scouting

app/services/rotations.py · tag counts Nov 27

Lineup data. The NBA's lineup table (lineups_base, this season) keeps only the 2,000 five-man lineups with the most minutes league-wide. On Nov 27 that is every lineup with about 3 minutes or more, 45 to 85 per team. Rarely used lineups are not in it. The Rotations tab shows the opponent's 10 lineups with the most minutes together: minutes, games, plus-minus and plus-minus per 48. Under 50 minutes together is a small sample, and a lineup with a player who is Out (official report or the lineup editor) is flagged. These are season numbers; the Last 10 toggle does not apply.

Tags use positions (G, F or C from the matchup data) and season ratings. A player without enough minutes for ratings never counts as a shooter or defender. Tags describe who is on the floor, not how the lineup has played.

TagRuleTop 10 lineups of all 30 teams with it (Nov 27)
StartersExactly the team's most common starting five this season30 of 300 (one per team)
Small ballNo player listed at center46
3pt4 or more players rated 60+ in Three-point shooting30
Defensiveaverage over the five of max(Perimeter defense, Interior defense) >= 75, with ratings for at least four59 (a first try at 60 tagged 208 of 300)
Big2 or more players listed at center5

Counters come from our healthy roster: players not Out under the lineup editor's statuses, with 100+ minutes this season or 500+ last season. Each counter has at most one center (the Big counter allows two) and one to three guards.

Their tagOur counter
StartersOur most common starting five, with Out and Doubtful players replaced by the healthy players with the most minutes
Small ballOur five best by Perimeter defense
3ptour five best by (Perimeter defense + Speed) / 2
Defensiveour five best by (Three-point shooting + Pull-up shooting + Playmaking + Finishing) / 4

Limits. Counters are simple rules on season ratings, not a model, and they do not look at how our lineups have played together. A first version picked five-guard counters, which is why guards are capped at three. "Use this lineup" loads a counter into the Lineups tab, where the matchup model judges it.
